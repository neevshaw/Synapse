Lesson 1.2: History of Artificial Intelligence

The concept of AI dates back to ancient times, with myths and stories of intelligent automata. The formal field of AI was established in 1956 at the Dartmouth Conference, where pioneers like John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, and Allen Newell outlined the vision for intelligent machines.

Example: Early AI programs like the Logic Theorist (1955) could solve mathematical problems, laying the groundwork for AI research.

Over the decades, AI has experienced periods of optimism, followed by “AI winters” due to funding cuts and slow progress. Breakthroughs in machine learning, neural networks, and data availability in the 21st century have led to modern AI applications, from voice assistants to autonomous vehicles.

Key historical milestones:

  • 1956 – Dartmouth Conference: Birth of AI as an academic discipline.
  • 1966 – ELIZA chatbot: Early natural language processing experiment.
  • 1980s – Expert systems: AI used for decision-making in businesses.
  • 2010s – Deep learning revolution: Neural networks outperform traditional AI methods.

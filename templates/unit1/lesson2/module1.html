Lesson 2.1: What is Machine Learning?

Machine Learning (ML) is a subset of AI that allows systems to learn from data and improve their performance without being explicitly programmed for every task.

Example: Spam filters in email services automatically detect unwanted emails by learning patterns from thousands of previous messages.

Unlike traditional programming, where humans write explicit rules, ML systems identify patterns in data and make predictions or decisions. This makes ML ideal for tasks that are too complex for rule-based systems, like image recognition, speech processing, and predictive analytics.

ML can be categorized by how systems learn:

  • Supervised Learning: Learns from labeled data (e.g., predicting house prices from historical sales).
  • Unsupervised Learning: Finds hidden patterns in unlabeled data (e.g., customer segmentation).
  • Reinforcement Learning: Learns by trial and error through rewards (e.g., AI agents in games or robotics).

Tip: Machine learning is everywhere — recommendation systems, voice assistants, fraud detection, and even self-driving cars all rely on it.

ML pipelines typically involve data collection, feature engineering, model training, and evaluation. Each step is crucial to build accurate, reliable systems. Errors in data or biased training examples can lead to poor predictions, which is why understanding the data is as important as the algorithms themselves.
